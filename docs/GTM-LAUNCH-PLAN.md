# BRKAR: first families, then a wider launch

Working plan, 5 October 2026. The founder selected Arabic-speaking Muslim MENA families as the first audience. Recruit a small cross-market cohort through reachable adult communities, rather than launching paid campaigns across the entire region. Arabic leads; English and French support multilingual households. No ads, outreach or expenditure have been initiated by this plan.

Execution has started with the [task handoff](EXECUTION-HANDOFF.md), [trilingual invitation and moderator guide](launch/PILOT-KIT.md), [one-page pilot brief](launch/PILOT-ONE-PAGER.md), [four lab-accurate scripts](launch/REELS-SCRIPT-PACK.md), [organic content drafts](launch/SOCIAL-CONTENT-PACK.md), [researched partner prospects and unsent contact drafts](launch/PARTNER-PROSPECTS-2026-10-07.md), and [blank private-session tracker template](launch/pilot-session-tracker-template.csv). Videos still need recording and review. Public invitations still need operator/support facts. The first cohort is moderated usability testing on one family device per session; it does not open the later authenticated remote-family pilot. Review the first six sessions before expanding to ten.

## What we can offer today

An adult-supervised, free family pilot uses four interactive labs and a twelve-mission curriculum in Arabic, English and French. The landing page, family setup, Kid Home, privacy page, Parent Dashboard, lab catalogue and child result page also support all three languages. Answers stay in the same browser. There is no sign-in, email capture, cloud sync, billing or automated assessment. The remaining eight interactive labs and fluent-language/device review remain before a broader trial.

Positioning: **بِركار — مساحة لينمو التفكير.** A family space for asking, predicting, testing and explaining. Show an actual before/after experiment and the child's reasoning, using invented demonstration answers. Do not promise improved IQ, an attention cure, protection from all online harms, or measurable learning benefits before evaluation. Avoid shaming children, parents or conventional schools.

Start with parents of children aged 9–12 who can read the current prompts with adult support. This is a pilot recruitment focus within the product's broader 7–14 audience, not a new product age limit. Include different language preferences, reading levels and devices; later test younger and older groups separately. An educator and Muslim-family reviewer should evaluate the examples and reading load.

## Launch gates

Before inviting a public cohort, publish the operator identity and a real adult support contact; name a person responsible for concerns. Clarify what is saved locally and who can see it. Verify the chosen domain and brand rights before using brkar.com as an owned address. Keep the existing working Firebase URL until this is done.

Use direct, consent-based adult invitations for the first cohort. A later waitlist needs its own adult contact form, privacy notice, opt-out and deletion process. Do not add tracking pixels to child routes or collect a child's school, exact age, religion, diagnosis or contact details. Institutions need a separate agreed facilitation and data-handling process. See PILOT-RELEASE-CHECKLIST.md for the existing product boundary.

## Campaign: a question worth slowing down for

Arabic concept: **سؤال صغير. تفكير أعمق.**
French: **Une petite question. Une réflexion qui grandit.**
English: **A small question. Room to think.**

Use the same calm paper, ink and green visual identity as the product. Produce clean 9:16 demonstrations with legible captions away from platform controls. Use a real screen recording, an adult voice and simple framing. No rapid-cut montage, flashing captions, fake urgency, child testimonials or unreviewed religious quotations. Brand values are shown through truthful observations, fair decisions and careful improvement.

| Creative | Opening question | Demonstration | Adult action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Water | ماذا لو أخطأ الحساس؟ | Prediction, faulty reading, then a second source of evidence | Try one lab together |
| Traffic | هل يمكن لقرارين صحيحين أن يتعارضا؟ | Two green requests, conflict, safety rule | Ask the child to predict first |
| Parent evidence | بدل «كم حصلت؟»، اسأل «كيف عرفت؟» | Invented prediction beside test result and explanation | Review the explanation together |
| Family values | كيف نحافظ على الأمانة في تجربة لم تنجح؟ | Record a failed prediction honestly, then revise | Discuss what changed the child's mind |

Suggested 30-second structure: 0–5 seconds ask a concrete question; 5–12 show the choice; 12–20 show what happened; 20–26 explain the revised rule; 26–30 invite the parent to try one supervised lab. Pause long enough to read. These timings are production hypotheses to test.

## Six-week sequence

| Period | Work | Proposed evidence gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Support/operator details, four accurate demonstrations, moderator script, recruit 10 families | Every invitation explains local storage and adult supervision |
| Week 2 | Ten moderated family sessions; split reading needs and devices | Identify the three biggest barriers; fix serious errors before expanding |
| Weeks 3–4 | Second cohort of 20 families; publish useful parent content; invite voluntary return | At least 14 of 20 complete one lab; at least 8 return within seven days; at least 12 parents can explain what the dashboard shows |
| Week 5 | Compare channels and message variants; conduct five educator conversations | Find the channel producing completed sessions with manageable founder time |
| Week 6 | Share a factual pilot learning note; propose one small workshop partnership | Continue only if access, understanding and safety concerns are addressed |

The thresholds above are internal go/no-go hypotheses, not industry benchmarks or proof of cognitive improvement. Report exact counts and denominators; a small sample does not justify broad efficacy claims. A child stopping, asking for help or taking a break is not a failure.

## Measurement without child tracking

Maintain a separate, access-controlled pilot sheet using random family codes. Record invitation source, adult session attendance, experiment completion, explanation in the child's own words, parent understanding, optional seven-day return, and moderator time. Keep adult contacts separately with permission and a retention/deletion date. Do not copy children's verbatim free text into marketing or analytics records.

Primary metric: families who complete an experiment and can discuss why the result changed. Secondary metrics: voluntary return, parent understanding of local storage, support needs and referrals. For each channel report completed sessions / invited families and founder minutes / completed session. Likes, views and watch time are creative diagnostics only.

Adult-facing UTM links may identify the creative, but the current application does not log conversions. Do not describe visits as tracked activations. Initially ask adults how they found the pilot and record aggregate answers manually.

## Budget and monetization

Start with organic recruitment and existing contacts. The $10 infrastructure budget is separate from marketing; it is not an advertising allocation. Do not start recurring campaigns or creator commitments without a separately chosen marketing budget. If the founder later approves a small test, compare two creatives on one eligible platform, use a finite total/lifetime budget, and stop if no qualified adult inquiries emerge. Verify platform minimums; a tiny budget may not produce interpretable results.

Keep this pilot free. After evidence of repeat family value and a working adult account flow, interview at least ten parents about willingness to pay and alternatives. Test a family subscription against a finite guided learning pack; select the model from actual use and support costs. Institutions may later buy facilitator support and curriculum access. No child-data monetization, child-facing ads, pay-to-win rewards or hidden renewal.

Price must cover payment fees, support time, hosting, content/review costs and applicable taxes. Billing, cancellation, refund terms and child access after cancellation require separate design and local review. No specific price or profitability forecast is established yet.
